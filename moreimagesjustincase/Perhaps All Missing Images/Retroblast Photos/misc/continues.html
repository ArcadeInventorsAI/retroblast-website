Editorial: The "Continue?"-ing Evil...

If you've visited this site often enough, odds are you've already run across one of my rants about the evils of the "Continue Game" feature of many later arcade games. In many ways I blame this specific feature for the demise of the arcade. While it may not have been the only culprit in the death of arcade gaming, it certainly hastened the end of the glory days of the arcade.

What's a "Continue"?

First off, I guess I should explain what, exactly, is this feature that I so despise. A game "continue" is the ability to continue playing a game from where you left off by inserting another credit.

Many games, especially from the 1990's on, include this feature. You lose your last life, and the game pops up a "Continue?" message with a count-down. If you pump in another credit, you get to continue right where you left off.

Okay...so what? Why is this bad, you may be asking? Well, let me explain...

Who Really Benefits?

First off, why even have a continue feature? What are the benefits of such a thing? Well, it honestly boils down to money — the operator's money.

A continue allows the game to collect more money from players, and the collection actually increases as the player gets deeper into the game: after all, the thinking goes, you've already "invested" a lot to get this far, why not pump in a few more quarters and see the end?

Games actually became designed to "force" deaths at certain stages, just to encourage a continue. Take, for example, the nearly impossible bosses on games such as SmashTV and Total Carnage. These are "quarter suckers," designed to suddenly ramp up the skill level of a game, perhaps only for that one battle.

But what about the benefits to the player? What about being able to continue beyond the levels you could reach through skill alone?

The Evil of Instant Gratification

On the surface, it seems like a nice benefit to the player: if you can't reach the end of a game, you can always still see what lies ahead by pumping another quarter. What's the harm, after all, it's your quarter?

Here's where I see the evil: by adding the ability to continue the game without needing skill to keep going, you've just pulled the rug out from under the entire "gaming" aspect of the experience. You've just turned an arcade game into a semi-interactive "pay as you go" movie.

Killing the Mystery and the Pride Factor

Besides just being fun, arcade games have two strong lures: the mystery of what lies on the next level, and the pride of achieving a high score. The "continue" feature destroys both of these rewards.

You probably remember something like this from your gaming days: the rumor that the last level in your favorite game had some ultra-cool monster or weapon, or that there was some amazing graphics display when you whupped the aliens on level 255. Remember how that motivated you to keep trying, even when the game was kicking your behind?

Well, with continues, you don't need to try harder anymore. Just get some more money, and the game will share all its secrets with you. Where's the achivement in that, and why would you care to return to the game once you've seen all there is to offer?

Likewise, if anyone with a large enough pocket can buy their way to the end of the game, what benefit do "skillz" have any more? The most bumbling game player can suddenly experience all the content that a skilled player can, he just has to pay more.

And how does this affect high scores? Some games wipe out scores with each new quarter, but if you're just dying to see the end of the game, you don't care about scores. And some games don't penalize a continue at all. Your score, in the end, just reflects how much you were willing to pay.

The "pride factor" is a major motivating force in arcade gaming. Those three little initials on the high-score table are what avid gamers play for, and that's what keeps them coming back again and again to the same machine.

Strip away the pride of achievement, and operators get a short term windfall as players pump in quarters to see the end of a game, followed by a sharp dropoff in income once players have seen all there is to see and move on to other games. The operators have taken the "quick payout" over the long-term income.

Will Power vs. The Quick and Easy

Okay, I realize that this ranting may seem a bit one-sided, and that with a little will-power, anyone can avoid using the continues. But let's be honest: how many kids are going to be able to resist such a quick and easy way to get through a game? And MAME only makes things worse, as continues are absolutely free!

My kids have quickly burned through games in MAME like Caveman Ninja simply by frantically pushing the credit buttons to get through the game. Their game skills haven't improved at all, as there is no real incentive to work harder to survive in a game when you can't really die.

The balance between operator income and gamer's enjoyment was disturbed by the continues, and both sides saw a short-term benefit at the expense of longer-term gameplay. The games became shooting stars in the arcades, quick and disposable, to be pumped full of quarters once and then never played again.

In the end, I'm glad my favorite games do not have continues: I'm not strong enough to resist the quick and easy path to the dark side. I don't care if it takes me the rest of my life to reach the last level on Robotron: when I get there, I'll know I earned it.

The Game is Never Over...

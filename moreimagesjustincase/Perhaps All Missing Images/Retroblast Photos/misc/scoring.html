Editorial: How to Score

Playing a lot of video games can get you thinking about some pretty esoteric things, silly little things that no one in their right mind would care about. Well, I care. What is this stupid little thing that I find myself caring so much about? Scoring.

No, not that kind — the videogame kind of scoring. Scoring systems, specifically. How games keep track of your accomplishments during gameplay, and reward you for those gaming achievements.

The Magical 10,000 Point Barrier

Videogaming has never had an "official" scoring system: each game invents its own set of point scores for various tasks achieved during gameplay. However, one informal standard arose fairly early in arcade gaming: the 10,000 point extra man/ship/shooter.

Even this standard, however, was only loosely adhered to: Centipede required 12,000 points, Robotron 25,000, and so on. Still many games had a magical 10,000 point "marker" to gauge your achivements. Many games even seemingly "inflated" their scoring system to ensure that they kept to the 10,000 point system, with minimum scoring for shooting a target set to something like 100 points.

A Point Should Mean Something

To me, each point in a scoring system should mean something. Why have a zero digit if it's never used? The unused zero started off in the world of pinball, where the zero wheel on the old electro-mechanical pinball machines was actually just a sticker. This "useless zero" tradition migrated over to the videogame world, at least for a great many games. It's impossible to score just one point in many games.

The "useless zero" tradition of pinball continues to this day, and has even gone to ridiculous extremes in some cases. Take, for example, my Bally "Attack from Mars" pinball machine: just hitting a pop-bumper is worth 1,000,000 points, minimum! A mediocre score for an Attack from Mars game is a measly 3,000,000,000 points.


Attack from Mars: The Ultimate High Scoring Game?

Even favorite video games of mine like Asteroids have a "useless zero" in their scoring: the lowest score on the game is a large asteroid for 20 points. Why not just make it 1 point? What's the point (ahem) of having scores start in the tens when there are no single-digit scoring opportunites?

Most, if not all "real games" (i.e., sports) have single point scoring. If there's no way to score one point, why have a two-point scoring opportunity? The minimum achievement in a game should always be the baseline: in other words, one point.

Scoring Done Right

I realize I'm being the king of the nit-pickers here, but arcade game scoring systems have always been a pet-peeve of mine. There are, however, a few shining examples of what I consider "perfect" scoring systems.

One of those games is Centipede (and its sequel Millipede). In Centipede, a point is worth something: a destroyed mushroom. Granted, it's not much, but it's a point. You also get 5 points for every damaged mushroom at the end of a life, another single-digit reward for your actions, and there is even a two-digit scoring opportunity, 10 points for a centipede segment.

Now that's how it's done: there are scoring opportunities ranging from a single digit to 4-digits, each one a fair gauge of the difficulty of achieving that opportunity. Tempest is another example of "full-digit scoring," with single digit scoring opportunities (the spikes are worth 1 point for each hit).

Even my favorite game, Robotron: 2084, uses the single-digit column, although only as part of 25 point targets (shots from Enforcers, Tanks, and Brains). Not perfect, but I'll forgive it this slight transgression.

Does This Really Matter?

Does any of this rambling rant about game scoring really matter? No, I'll be the first to admit this is just my own pet peeve, and perhaps it says too much about my own personality that I care so much about how video games keep score.

Still, a well-planned scoring system is reflective of how much care and thought went into a game's design. While none of this will affect my enjoyment of classic video games, it felt good to get this off my chest, and I award myself 1 point.

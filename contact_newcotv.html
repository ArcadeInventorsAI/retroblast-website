About This Site

RetroBlast! is a place for news and reviews about all things related to retrogaming. What's retrogaming, you ask? Well, thanks to a dedicated group of hobbists, programmers, and preservationists, it is still possible to play practically any video game ever created, even games that were written for computer or arcade systems that are no longer available (thanks to the use of emulators, which recreate virtually the old hardware on more modern computing equipment).

Other enthusiasts have worked to preserve the original pinball machines and other coin-operated machines once found in the arcades. Retrogaming, at least for me, is a never-ending quest to reproduce the joy of playing those old videogames, pinball machines, and other coin-operated entertainments as they were originally experienced in the arcades, whether through preservation or emulation.

RetroBlast is designed as a resource for the home arcade enthusiast — there are reviews to help with purchase decisions, “How-To” articles, retrogaming news, and more. Through both written reviews and videos, RetroBlast is designed to help both the novice and the expert retrogamer build the gameroom of their dreams.

 

Kevin Steele
Founder

Kevin is the owner and publisher of GameRoom Magazine, and an avid retrogaming enthusiast, particularly of arcade video games and pinballs. He created RetroBlast nearly three years ago as a way to document and share his experiences in his never-ending quest to create the perfect "home arcade," full of both classic arcade and pinball games as well as modern reproductions of the arcade experience.

Kevin's current home arcade consists of the MAMEframe II, a MAME arcade cabinet capable of playing over 4,000 arcade videogames, a Quasicade 2 arcade system (an advanced arcade cabinet designed for consoles such as the XBox), a 1996 Bally "Attack from Mars" pinball, a 1993 Bally "Twilight Zone" pinball, a 1992 Bally “Doctor Who” pinball, and several Pachinko and Pachislo machines. More machines are inevitable.

James McGovern
Managing Editor

James rediscovered MAME a little over two years ago and was completely blown away by the custom cabinets being built then and now by the classic gaming community. He knew he was hooked very shortly after seeing the amazing projects featured on the Build Your Own Arcade Controls website. In the early days of researching products for his arcade cabinet, he soon found the RetroBlast pages. Here Jamexs found an enormous amount of information that helped him navigate the complexities of making his own home arcade system possible. During that time James wrote a review of the Defender tribute game, Guardian, for the pages of RetroBlast.

Since then James has accumulated a few games of his own with more on the way all the time. He currently has a dedicated Tron upright, and an Omega Race that hosts MAME, but will be restored in the near future. James also has numerous projects in various states of "completion" including a Vector MAME system and a newly acquired dynamo cabinet that may play host to a fighter-style game at some point in the future. Also, in what appears to be a forming RetroBlast tradition James also has a Pachinko machine, but no pinball games. Yet.

Mitch Gerson
Contributing Editor

Growing up walking distance from Coney Island in Brooklyn it was pretty much a given that Mitch would end up loving video games. Having spent countless hours pumping quarters into the boardwalk arcades and buying the various consoles over the years he still dreamt of someday owning a real arcade machine. Having discovered MAME™ about 3 years ago his new hobby grew into a true obsession and Mitch is now the proud owner of a kit built cocktail machine, rebuilt Stargate stand up (both running MAME™) and now more recently 3 Japanese Pachislo Slot machines (Metal Slug, Tekken and Jet Set Radio).

Mitch discovered that he enjoys building, modding and adjusting his MAME™ machines sometimes even more than playing the games themselves!

Mitch discovered Kevin’s RetroBlast site when he launched back in July of 2003 and had really enjoyed his how-to video’s and product reviews. Before Mitch knew it he found himself submitting story ideas, links and eventually articles and he hopes to keep contributing to RetroBlast for a long time to come.
